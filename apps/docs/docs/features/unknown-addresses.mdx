Decide what happens to mail sent to any address on your <Terminology title="domain whose catch-all points at the Worker" to="/docs/reference/terminology#base-domain">base domain</Terminology> that isn't one of your aliases.

## Why Use This Feature?

1. Rejecting unknown addresses tells senders the address doesn't exist so guessed addresses get a clear bounce.
2. Dropping unknown addresses gives spammers no signal about which addresses are real.
3. Forwarding unknown addresses lets you make up an address on the spot and still receive the mail.

## Options

Set `unknown_alias_action` in the <Terminology title="config.json file with destinations, aliases, and settings" to="/docs/reference/terminology#config">config</Terminology>, or choose it under **Settings** in the `mm` menu.

| Value     | What happens                                               |
|-----------|------------------------------------------------------------|
| `reject`  | The sender gets a bounce saying the address was not found. |
| `drop`    | The message is accepted and silently discarded.            |
| `forward` | The message is forwarded to `default_destination`.         |

```json title="config.json"
{
  "settings": {
    "unknown_alias_action": "forward",
    "default_destination": "personal"
  }
}
```

The <Terminology title="what happens to mail for non-alias addresses" to="/docs/reference/terminology#unknown-alias-action">unknown alias action</Terminology> applies because the <Terminology title="Email Routing rule for addresses without their own rule" to="/docs/reference/terminology#catch-all">catch-all</Terminology> sends every address without its own Email Routing rule to the <Terminology title="Cloudflare Worker that routes your mail" to="/docs/reference/terminology#worker">Worker</Terminology>.

## Refusals Never Fall Through

A known <Terminology title="address on your domain that forwards mail" to="/docs/reference/terminology#alias">alias</Terminology> that is disabled, expired, or refuses a sender never forwards to the default <Terminology title="verified real inbox that aliases forward to" to="/docs/reference/terminology#destination">destination</Terminology>. That way, turning off an <Terminology title="address on your domain that forwards mail" to="/docs/reference/terminology#alias">alias</Terminology> really stops its mail, even with `forward` selected.

| Setting   | What a refusal does      |
|-----------|--------------------------|
| `reject`  | Bounces with the reason. |
| `drop`    | Discards silently.       |
| `forward` | Bounces with the reason. |

## Other Domains

Mail for a domain other than your <Terminology title="domain whose catch-all points at the Worker" to="/docs/reference/terminology#base-domain">base domain</Terminology> is always rejected. This can happen if you route another domain's catch-all at the same <Terminology title="Cloudflare Worker that routes your mail" to="/docs/reference/terminology#worker">Worker</Terminology>.

:::tip
`drop` keeps spammers guessing, but a person who mistypes an address also gets no bounce. It is recommended to pick `reject` unless spam to random addresses is a real problem for you.
:::
