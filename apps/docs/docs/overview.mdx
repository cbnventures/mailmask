Give every site you sign up for its own address on your domain, and forward the mail to your real inbox without handing that inbox to anyone.

## What Is This?

**Mailmask** turns a domain you already have on Cloudflare into a private alias service. Each <Terminology title="A stand-in address; mail still lands in your real inbox" to="/docs/reference/terminology#aliases-and-mail-flow">alias</Terminology> forwards to a real inbox you control, called a <Terminology title="Your real inbox, where an alias's mail ends up" to="/docs/reference/terminology#aliases-and-mail-flow">destination</Terminology>.

The forwarding runs on a Cloudflare <Terminology title="Mailmask itself, running on your Cloudflare account" to="/docs/reference/terminology#cloudflare-and-deploy">Worker</Terminology> in your own account, and mail is never stored. You manage everything through an interactive CLI that also deploys the <Terminology title="Mailmask itself, running on your Cloudflare account" to="/docs/reference/terminology#cloudflare-and-deploy">Worker</Terminology> for you.

## Why Use Mailmask?

1. Gives every sign-up a unique address so a leaked or sold address points straight back to the site that leaked it.
2. Lets an <Terminology title="A stand-in address; mail still lands in your real inbox" to="/docs/reference/terminology#aliases-and-mail-flow">alias</Terminology> expire on its own so a free trial or one-time download can't keep emailing you.
3. Accepts mail only from the senders you expect so an address that ends up on a spam list stays quiet.
4. Switches an <Terminology title="A stand-in address; mail still lands in your real inbox" to="/docs/reference/terminology#aliases-and-mail-flow">alias</Terminology> off in seconds so you can cut off a noisy sender without changing your real address.
5. Runs on your own Cloudflare account so no third-party alias provider sits in the middle of your mail.

## Key Capabilities

- **Aliases on demand** — Generate memorable (`velvet-harbor-42`) or random (`k7mq2xv9pa`) addresses, or pick your own. Each enabled alias takes one Email Routing rule, and Cloudflare allows 200 per domain, while disabled or reserved aliases need no rule.
- **Expiring aliases** — Give an <Terminology title="A stand-in address; mail still lands in your real inbox" to="/docs/reference/terminology#aliases-and-mail-flow">alias</Terminology> a lifetime like `30m`, `7d`, or a date, and it stops accepting mail on its own.
- **Sender rules** — Allow only specific senders or domains per <Terminology title="A stand-in address; mail still lands in your real inbox" to="/docs/reference/terminology#aliases-and-mail-flow">alias</Terminology>, and block the ones that abuse it.
- **One-switch shutdown** — Disable an <Terminology title="A stand-in address; mail still lands in your real inbox" to="/docs/reference/terminology#aliases-and-mail-flow">alias</Terminology> without deleting it, so the address stays reserved.
- **Subaddressing** — `alias+anything@` reaches the same <Terminology title="A stand-in address; mail still lands in your real inbox" to="/docs/reference/terminology#aliases-and-mail-flow">alias</Terminology>.
- **Refusal notifications** — Alert Discord, Slack, Telegram, ntfy, email, webhooks, and other supported services when an expired or filtered <Terminology title="A stand-in address; mail still lands in your real inbox" to="/docs/reference/terminology#aliases-and-mail-flow">alias</Terminology> turns mail away.
- **Automatic Email Routing setup** — Deploying registers your inboxes and keeps a <Terminology title="Sends one alias's mail to Mailmask" to="/docs/reference/terminology#cloudflare-and-deploy">routing rule</Terminology> for each enabled <Terminology title="A stand-in address; mail still lands in your real inbox" to="/docs/reference/terminology#aliases-and-mail-flow">alias</Terminology> that sends its mail to the <Terminology title="Mailmask itself, running on your Cloudflare account" to="/docs/reference/terminology#cloudflare-and-deploy">Worker</Terminology>, on an apex or a subdomain.

## How It Works

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    Sender[Sender] --> Routing[Cloudflare Email Routing]
    Routing --> Known{Enabled alias?}
    Known --> KnownNo([No]):::muted --> Unknown[Cloudflare rejects: Address does not exist]
    Known --> KnownYes([Yes]):::muted --> Worker[Mailmask Worker]
    Worker --> Active{Not expired, sender allowed?}
    Active --> ActiveYes([Yes]):::muted --> Inbox[Your real inbox]
    Active --> ActiveNo([No]):::muted --> Refuse[Reject with the reason]
```

When someone emails `velvet-harbor-42@example.com`, Cloudflare hands the message to your <Terminology title="Mailmask itself, running on your Cloudflare account" to="/docs/reference/terminology#cloudflare-and-deploy">Worker</Terminology>. The <Terminology title="Mailmask itself, running on your Cloudflare account" to="/docs/reference/terminology#cloudflare-and-deploy">Worker</Terminology> looks up the <Terminology title="A stand-in address; mail still lands in your real inbox" to="/docs/reference/terminology#aliases-and-mail-flow">alias</Terminology>, checks that it is enabled, not expired, and that the sender is allowed, then forwards the message to its <Terminology title="Your real inbox, where an alias's mail ends up" to="/docs/reference/terminology#aliases-and-mail-flow">destination</Terminology>.

Mail for an <Terminology title="A stand-in address; mail still lands in your real inbox" to="/docs/reference/terminology#aliases-and-mail-flow">alias</Terminology> that doesn't pass those checks is turned away instead of reaching your inbox. Nothing is written to disk along the way.

Mail for an address that isn't an enabled <Terminology title="A stand-in address; mail still lands in your real inbox" to="/docs/reference/terminology#aliases-and-mail-flow">alias</Terminology> has no <Terminology title="Sends one alias's mail to Mailmask" to="/docs/reference/terminology#cloudflare-and-deploy">routing rule</Terminology>, so Cloudflare rejects it before it reaches the <Terminology title="Mailmask itself, running on your Cloudflare account" to="/docs/reference/terminology#cloudflare-and-deploy">Worker</Terminology>.

:::info
Mailmask is forward-only. You receive mail at an <Terminology title="A stand-in address; mail still lands in your real inbox" to="/docs/reference/terminology#aliases-and-mail-flow">alias</Terminology>, but replies you send come from your real address, so it is recommended to reply only when you're comfortable revealing it.
:::

## What You Will Need

- **Node.js** — v22, v24, or v26.
- **Cloudflare account** — The free plan is enough.
- **A domain on Cloudflare** — With <Terminology title="Cloudflare's mail intake that hands messages to Mailmask" to="/docs/reference/terminology#cloudflare-and-deploy">Cloudflare Email Routing</Terminology> enabled, which adds the mail records for you.
- **A real inbox** — Any address you can read, such as Gmail or Fastmail, to receive forwarded mail.
